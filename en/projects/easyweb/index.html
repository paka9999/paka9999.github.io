Why

Sign-up and posting screens with many fields are a wall for seniors. This Electron app shows the real site as-is and fills it in from one large-type form on the left. It is a personal aid used with my own accounts, not something to distribute.

Rules kept in the design

  • A human presses the final submit. Irreversible buttons like “complete sign-up” or “publish” are a handoff step; the app only marks them in orange. Undoing a wrong post is much harder.
  • CAPTCHAs, identity verification and terms consent are never automated. All of it goes back to the person.
  • Passwords are never stored in plain text. They are encrypted with safeStorage (DPAPI on Windows), and if encryption is unavailable, saving is refused. Progress logs are masked too.
  • Recipes live outside the code. When a site changes, only the JSON changes.
  • On failure, capture the screen right then. “Stopped at step X” plus a view-screen button.

Structure

A 460px control panel (large-type form) on the left and the real site (WebContentsView) on the right. main/automation.js is the recipe engine; recipes are read from recipes/*.json or the user folder.

Status

One working recipe: sign-up on a community site built on a Korean bulletin-board engine. Across its two steps (terms → form), terms, CAPTCHA and the final submit are handed to the person.

Summary

An Electron app that fills in form-heavy pages from a single large-type form. The final submit, CAPTCHAs and identity checks are handed to the person; passwords live only in the OS keychain (safeStorage). Site behavior is defined in JSON recipes.

  • Irreversible buttons are a handoff step: the app only highlights them in orange
  • CAPTCHAs, identity verification and terms consent are never automated
  • On failure it captures the screen at that moment: "stopped at step X"
