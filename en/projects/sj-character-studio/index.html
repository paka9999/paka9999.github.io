How it works

1. Build 3D candidates from reference art

I keep reference art, existing GLBs and their variants together in the library. To build a new candidate, the local Pixal3D workflow in ComfyUI generates a textured mesh that I load as a GLB.

For Serin, I kept the source art, seed and quality setting fixed and changed the input views. The single-view model was 46% as deep as it was tall. The three-view model matched the reference at 25%. This was a test on one character.

2. Compare and refine at matching angles

I match the A/B camera and lighting to locate defects. A brush follows connected mesh surfaces, with protection masks over areas like the eyes. The tool blends nearby colors in the selected region. I save a separate GLB and reopen it to verify the result.

Lumi’s eyes and mouth lost detail because the face was small in the full-body image. I generated a separate face from a tighter crop, joined it to the head and body, and checked the seams and face at fixed angles.

3. Check the skeleton and skin weights

I generate skeletons and skin weights with SkinTokens and try raised-arm poses and walking. I check whether sleeves follow the arms, clothing sticks to the wrong body part, or faces tear.

I rebuilt Lumi from T-pose art with the arms separated from the body. The arm test went from 207 torn faces to 111. The remaining deformation stays flagged.

4. Edit keyframes, IK and hit timing

On the timeline I play an existing clip and edit bone position and rotation keys. Two-bone arm IK moves the hand toward a target and bakes the pose to keys. Untouched bones keep their original animation.

The sword’s base and tip drive the trail. The Effekseer hit plays at the socket position for the chosen frame. I can scrub backward and inspect the same moment again.

5. Export and verify in Unity

I export the reviewed GLB and motion data. For selected models I convert to FBX through Blender and import into Unity. Unity initially treated a Lumi sleeve bone as a hand, so I mapped the Humanoid avatar using Studio’s bone names.

In Unity I applied arm-lowering and walking poses again to verify limb movement. I recorded what was checked in the viewer and what was verified in the engine.

6. Record what improved and what still fails

In the November 15 Lumi test I reprojected color onto surfaces visible from the reference image. Eyebrows and collar color moved closer to the reference. The iris became too bright, and the side profile and ear geometry still needed work. The matched views recorded where to investigate next.

Project scope

I designed and built this independent personal tool to take characters from concept art through 3D generation, refinement and import into the game engine. I have checked save-and-reopen and Unity import on selected candidates. Consistent generation from one reference across characters and per-model mobile testing are still in progress.

Checking the same poses in the viewer and Unity

I used the same comparison poses in the Three.js workbench and in Unity Humanoid. I updated the Blender FBX export to preserve textures and finger-bone names, then checked material and bone mapping after import. A mesh that looked fine at rest could tear at the sleeves during a walk or an arm raise, so I saved comparison views and notes for each generation and rigging pass.

Summary

I built the tool to turn concept art into 3D characters for games. I compare and refine mesh and texture candidates, check skeletal motion, and export selected models as FBX. I tested their movement with Unity Humanoid.

  • Matched-angle comparison and separate saves for refinement candidates
  • Keyframe and IK previews with GLB reopen checks
  • FBX export and Unity Humanoid bone mapping
