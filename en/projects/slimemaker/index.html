Why

An ASMR game where you press, grab and stretch a wobbling slime with sound. The constraint: no external assets or audio, everything generated at runtime.

What I built

  • Soft body: 72 boundary points tied with springs; press and it dents, release and it wobbles back.
  • Six slimes: Crunch, soap, pudding, butter, cloud, jelly. Color, gloss, viscosity and sound all differ.
  • Procedural ASMR sound: Synthesized with no audio files: noise grains for crunch, water-drop sine sweeps for jelly.
  • No scene editing: Camera, UI, audio and slime are bootstrapped from code with RuntimeInitializeOnLoadMethod.

Mobile layout

Designed around width being the fixed constraint in a portrait game: a 1080×1920 reference with width-based scaling, top bar and bottom UI anchored to the safe area, a fixed 10-unit camera world width, and automatic slime shrinking on cramped devices to avoid overlap. Covers 9:16–9:21 phones with notches and punch-holes, and 3:4 tablets.

Stack

Unity 6 (6000.3), URP, New Input System. Mouse on PC, multi-touch on mobile.

Summary

Six soft-body slimes built from 72 boundary points on springs. Procedural ASMR sound synthesized at runtime with no audio files, zero external assets, and everything bootstrapped from code without editing the scene.

  • Six slimes with different color, gloss, viscosity and sound
  • Procedural sound: noise grains for crunch, water-drop sine sweeps for jelly
  • Covers 9:16 to 9:21 phones and 3:4 tablets with safe-area anchoring and width-based scaling
