Generating music for a game

I built this tool to compare winter workshop music for Jewelry Garden. A local Qwen model turns a short brief into arrangement notes, BPM, key and meter. ACE-Step generates the track. The presets offer different instrument combinations, such as felt piano or flute and nylon guitar.

Auditioning and exporting

The library keeps the original audio, its loop and the generation settings together. The player stays available when switching between generation and the library. I used FFmpeg to match the loudness of the reference and candidate, then added loop boundary adjustments, crossfades and WAV export.

Verification

I generated instrumental and vocal tracks on Windows and checked playback, mute, and original and loop downloads in the browser. The clip above is a sample instrumental made in Music Workshop.

Used in Jewelry Garden

I used music made in Music Workshop as background music in Jewelry Garden. I also brought sound effects made with the tool into the game.

Arrangements and section edits

I made the AI arrangement draft editable before generation. Reloading a track’s model, lyrics, structure and seed lets me compare a single change, such as replacing an instrument. When regenerating a section, I crossfade inside the selected range and preserve the original audio outside it. Rearrangements, section edits and extensions each save as a new take.

Summary

I built a local Windows tool to audition winter puzzle-game music. It brings generation, a track library, level-matched comparison, loop editing and WAV export into one workspace. I used its music and sound effects in Jewelry Garden.

  • Turns a short brief into instruments, tempo, key and meter for local ACE-Step generation
  • Keeps the original and loop versions and compares candidates with a reference at matched loudness
  • Supports editable lyric drafts, vocal tracks and WAV export after loop adjustment
